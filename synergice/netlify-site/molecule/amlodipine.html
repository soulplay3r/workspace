About Amlodipine

Amlodipine is a long-acting calcium channel blocker of the dihydropyridine class. It relaxes the smooth muscle in artery walls, widening blood vessels and lowering blood pressure, and it reduces the heart's oxygen demand in angina. Its long half-life allows smooth control with one dose a day. It is used for hypertension, chronic stable angina and vasospastic angina, often in combination with other medicines. It was first approved by the US FDA in July 1992.

Synergice manufactures amlodipine as tablets (as amlodipine besylate), in strengths of 5 mg, 10 mg. Clinical information

Mechanism of action

Amlodipine inhibits calcium influx through L-type voltage-gated calcium channels, acting more on vascular smooth muscle than on cardiac muscle. Peripheral arterial vasodilation lowers vascular resistance and blood pressure; in angina it reduces afterload and myocardial oxygen demand and relieves coronary spasm.

Indication

Amlodipine is indicated for the treatment of hypertension, alone or with other antihypertensive agents, for chronic stable angina and vasospastic (Prinzmetal's) angina, and to reduce the risk of hospitalisation for angina in patients with angiographically documented coronary artery disease.
