About Amantadine

Amantadine is an adamantane derivative with antiparkinsonian and antiviral activity. First approved by the US FDA in 1966 to prevent influenza A, it was found by chance in 1969 to improve Parkinson's disease, which is now its main use. It helps tremor, rigidity and slowness of movement and reduces levodopa-induced dyskinesia. It is excreted mostly unchanged by the kidneys, so doses are reduced in renal impairment.

Synergice manufactures amantadine as soft gelatin capsules and tablets, in strengths of 100 mg. Production takes place within the Strides Pharma Science manufacturing network under Good Manufacturing Practice, and every batch is released against its approved specification. The product is supplied to marketing authorisation holders, distributors and licensing partners; the forms, strengths and packs available for each market are confirmed on enquiry.

Clinical information

Mechanism of action

In Parkinson's disease amantadine acts as a weak non-competitive NMDA receptor antagonist and increases dopamine release while reducing its reuptake, with mild anticholinergic effects. Against influenza A it blocks the viral M2 proton channel, though most current strains are resistant.

Indication

Amantadine is indicated for Parkinson's disease and parkinsonism and for drug-induced extrapyramidal reactions. It is also labelled for influenza A prophylaxis and treatment, but public health agencies no longer recommend it for influenza because of widespread resistance.
