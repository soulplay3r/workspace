About Mycophenolate Mofetil

Mycophenolate mofetil is an oral immunosuppressant used to prevent organ rejection after transplantation. It is a prodrug of mycophenolic acid, which blocks an enzyme lymphocytes depend on to make the guanine nucleotides they need to multiply, so it suppresses the immune response to a transplanted organ. It is used with a calcineurin inhibitor and corticosteroids after kidney, heart and liver transplantation. It was first approved by the US FDA in May 1995.

Synergice manufactures mycophenolate Mofetil as tablets and capsules. Production takes place within the Strides Pharma Science manufacturing network under Good Manufacturing Practice, and every batch is released against its approved specification. The product is supplied to marketing authorisation holders, distributors and licensing partners; the forms, strengths and packs available for each market are confirmed on enquiry.

Clinical information

Mechanism of action

Mycophenolate mofetil is hydrolysed to mycophenolic acid, a selective, reversible, uncompetitive inhibitor of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase. This depletes guanosine nucleotides in T and B lymphocytes, which rely on de novo purine synthesis, producing a cytostatic effect and reduced antibody formation.

Indication

Mycophenolate mofetil is indicated for the prophylaxis of organ rejection in recipients of allogeneic kidney, heart or liver transplants, in combination with other immunosuppressants such as a calcineurin inhibitor and corticosteroids.
