About Metoprolol

Metoprolol is a beta-1 selective adrenergic blocker, a beta blocker that acts mainly on the heart. It slows the heart rate and reduces the force of contraction, lowering blood pressure and the heart's workload. It is used for hypertension and angina, after myocardial infarction and, as an extended-release form, in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. Metoprolol tartrate was first approved by the US FDA in 1978.

Synergice manufactures metoprolol as tablets (tartrate) and extended-release tablets (succinate), in strengths of 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg. Production takes place within the Strides Pharma Science manufacturing network under Good Manufacturing Practice, and every batch is released against its approved specification. The product is supplied to marketing authorisation holders, distributors and licensing partners; the forms, strengths and packs available for each market are confirmed on enquiry.

Clinical information

Mechanism of action

Metoprolol competitively blocks beta-1 adrenergic receptors in the heart, reducing heart rate, contractility and cardiac output, particularly during exercise or stress. It also reduces renin release. It has no intrinsic sympathomimetic activity, and its beta-1 selectivity diminishes at higher doses.

Indication

Metoprolol is indicated for hypertension and angina pectoris. Immediate-release metoprolol tartrate is indicated in acute myocardial infarction, and extended-release metoprolol succinate is indicated for stable, symptomatic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.
