About Cyclosporine

Cyclosporine, also known as ciclosporin, is a cyclic peptide immunosuppressant from the calcineurin inhibitor class, derived from the fungus Tolypocladium inflatum. It suppresses T-cell activation without broadly suppressing the bone marrow, and its introduction made routine organ transplantation possible. It is used to prevent rejection after kidney, liver and heart transplantation and, in microemulsion form, for severe psoriasis and rheumatoid arthritis. It was first approved by the US FDA in November 1983.

Synergice manufactures cyclosporine as soft gelatin capsules. Production takes place within the Strides Pharma Science manufacturing network under Good Manufacturing Practice, and every batch is released against its approved specification. The product is supplied to marketing authorisation holders, distributors and licensing partners; the forms, strengths and packs available for each market are confirmed on enquiry.

Clinical information

Mechanism of action

Cyclosporine binds cyclophilin inside T lymphocytes. The complex inhibits calcineurin, preventing activation of the transcription factor NFAT and blocking transcription of interleukin-2 and other early cytokines, so helper and cytotoxic T cells do not expand.

Indication

Cyclosporine is indicated for prophylaxis of organ rejection in kidney, liver and heart transplantation. The microemulsion form is also indicated for severe active rheumatoid arthritis not responding to methotrexate and for severe recalcitrant plaque psoriasis.
