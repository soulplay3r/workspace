About Naproxen Sodium

Naproxen sodium is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) of the propionic acid class. It relieves pain, reduces fever and dampens inflammation by blocking the production of prostaglandins. The sodium salt is absorbed faster than naproxen itself, and its long half-life allows dosing every 8 to 12 hours. It is used over the counter for minor aches, pains and fever and on prescription for arthritis. Naproxen was first approved by the US FDA in 1976.

Synergice manufactures naproxen Sodium as soft gelatin capsules (OTC), in strengths of 220 mg (OTC). Clinical information

Mechanism of action

Naproxen reversibly inhibits cyclooxygenase-1 and cyclooxygenase-2, reducing synthesis of prostaglandins that sensitise pain receptors, mediate inflammation and raise the hypothalamic temperature set point. COX-1 inhibition in the gut and platelets accounts for its gastrointestinal and bleeding risks.

Indication

Over the counter, naproxen sodium 220 mg is indicated for temporary relief of minor aches and pains and to reduce fever. Prescription naproxen is indicated for rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, tendonitis, bursitis, acute gout and primary dysmenorrhoea.
