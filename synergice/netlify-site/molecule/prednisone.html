About Prednisone

Prednisone is a synthetic glucocorticoid, or corticosteroid, taken by mouth. It is a prodrug that the liver converts to prednisolone, which suppresses inflammation and immune activity across many tissues. One molecule covers a very wide range of conditions, from asthma and arthritis to autoimmune disease, transplant rejection and some blood cancers, at doses from a few milligrams to high-dose courses. It was first approved by the US FDA in 1955.

Synergice manufactures prednisone as tablets, in strengths of 2.5 mg, 5 mg. Production takes place within the Strides Pharma Science manufacturing network under Good Manufacturing Practice, and every batch is released against its approved specification. The product is supplied to marketing authorisation holders, distributors and licensing partners; the forms, strengths and packs available for each market are confirmed on enquiry.

Clinical information

Mechanism of action

Prednisolone binds the cytoplasmic glucocorticoid receptor, which moves to the nucleus and switches on anti-inflammatory genes while repressing pro-inflammatory transcription factors such as NF-kappa-B and AP-1. Cytokine, prostaglandin and leukotriene production falls and immune cell migration is reduced.

Indication

Prednisone is indicated for a wide range of endocrine, rheumatic, collagen, dermatological, allergic, ophthalmic, respiratory, haematological, neoplastic, oedematous and gastrointestinal conditions that respond to glucocorticoids, including asthma, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus and nephrotic syndrome.
