About Nitrofurantoin

Nitrofurantoin is an oral antibacterial of the nitrofuran class used for bladder infections. It is cleared so quickly into urine that it reaches high concentrations in the bladder but very low levels in blood and tissues, which limits its effect on gut bacteria. Resistance among Escherichia coli remains low after seven decades of use, and guidelines recommend it first-line for uncomplicated cystitis. It was first approved by the US FDA in 1953.

Clinical information

Mechanism of action

Nitrofurantoin is reduced by bacterial flavoproteins to reactive intermediates that attack ribosomal proteins, DNA, RNA and enzymes of energy metabolism and cell wall synthesis. Acting on many targets at once makes resistance from single mutations uncommon.

Indication

Nitrofurantoin is indicated for the treatment of urinary tract infections caused by susceptible strains of Escherichia coli, enterococci, Staphylococcus aureus and certain Klebsiella and Enterobacter species. It is not indicated for pyelonephritis or perinephric abscesses.
