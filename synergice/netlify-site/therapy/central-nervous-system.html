About this therapy area

Central nervous system (CNS) medicines are chronic-use products where patients and prescribers value consistency. Several, including methylphenidate, are controlled substances, so partners need manufacturing sites and logistics that can handle quota, import and export permits under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances.

This section covers amantadine, an older molecule whose second life in Parkinson's disease was discovered by chance, and methylphenidate, the most widely used stimulant for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD).

Molecule summaries

Amantadine

Amantadine is an adamantane derivative first approved by the US FDA in 1966 to prevent influenza A. Its benefit in Parkinson's disease was discovered by chance in 1969, and it is now used mainly for Parkinson's disease and drug-induced extrapyramidal reactions, where it acts as an NMDA receptor antagonist and increases dopamine release.

Methylphenidate

Methylphenidate is a central nervous system stimulant that blocks dopamine and noradrenaline transporters, raising the levels of both neurotransmitters in the prefrontal cortex and striatum. Synthesised in 1944 and approved by the US FDA in 1955 as Ritalin, it is a first-line treatment for ADHD and is used for narcolepsy.
