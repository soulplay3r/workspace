About this therapy area

Oral oncology medicines have changed how many cancers are treated, moving long courses of therapy from infusion chairs to patients' homes. For partners, oral oncology products bring specific requirements: containment during manufacture, cytotoxic handling in the supply chain, and close attention to bioequivalence because the therapeutic margin is narrow.

The oncology molecules listed here include two aromatase inhibitors used in hormone receptor-positive breast cancer (letrozole, anastrozole), an oral prodrug of 5-fluorouracil used in colorectal and breast cancer (capecitabine), and the first targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor, imatinib, which turned chronic myeloid leukaemia into a manageable chronic condition.

Molecule summaries

Anastrozole

Anastrozole is a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor that suppresses estrogen synthesis in postmenopausal women. Approved by the US FDA in December 1995 as Arimidex, it is a standard adjuvant and advanced-disease treatment for hormone receptor-positive breast cancer.

Capecitabine

Capecitabine is an oral prodrug that is converted to 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) in three enzymatic steps, the last of them concentrated in tumour tissue. Approved by the US FDA in April 1998 as Xeloda, it is used for colorectal and breast cancer and gives patients an oral alternative to infused 5-FU.

Imatinib

Imatinib is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor that blocks the BCR-ABL fusion protein driving chronic myeloid leukaemia, as well as KIT and PDGFR. Approved by the US FDA in May 2001 as Gleevec, it was the first targeted kinase inhibitor and transformed CML into a long-term manageable disease.

Letrozole

Letrozole is a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor that lowers estrogen levels by blocking the enzyme that converts androgens to estrogens. Approved by the US FDA in July 1997 as Femara, it is used to treat hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women.
