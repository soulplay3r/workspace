About this therapy area

Malaria still causes hundreds of thousands of deaths every year, most of them among young children in sub-Saharan Africa. Artemisinin-based combination therapies (ACTs) are the WHO-recommended first-line treatment for uncomplicated Plasmodium falciparum malaria, and much of the global supply is procured through public tenders and donor programmes.

Artemether-lumefantrine is the most widely used ACT. It pairs a fast-acting artemisinin derivative with a long-acting partner drug, so the parasite load drops quickly and the residual parasites are cleared over the following days.

Molecule summaries

Artemether / Lumefantrine

Artemether-lumefantrine is an artemisinin-based combination therapy that pairs fast-acting artemether with long-acting lumefantrine to treat uncomplicated Plasmodium falciparum malaria. First registered in 1999 and approved by the US FDA in April 2009 as Coartem, it is the most widely used malaria treatment in the world.

Frequently asked questions

Why are anti-malarials given as combinations?

Combining two drugs with different mechanisms and half-lives clears parasites faster and makes it much harder for resistance to emerge to either drug. WHO recommends against artemisinin monotherapy for this reason.

Who buys anti-malarial finished products?

Most anti-malarials are procured by national malaria programmes, the Global Fund and other donor-funded buyers, which usually require WHO prequalification or a stringent regulatory approval.
