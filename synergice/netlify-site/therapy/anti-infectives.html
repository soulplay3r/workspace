About this therapy area

Antimicrobial resistance has made older antibiotics with low resistance rates more valuable, not less. Molecules that act at a single site of infection and spare the gut flora are now preferred by stewardship guidelines because they treat the infection with less pressure on resistance.

Nitrofurantoin is a good example. It has been in use since the 1950s, reaches therapeutic concentrations almost only in urine, and is recommended as a first-line treatment for uncomplicated cystitis in many guidelines.

Molecule summaries

Nitrofurantoin

Nitrofurantoin is a nitrofuran antibacterial that is concentrated in urine and used to treat and prevent uncomplicated lower urinary tract infections. First approved by the US FDA in 1953, it remains a first-line treatment for cystitis because resistance to it is still uncommon.

Frequently asked questions

Which anti-infective molecules are listed?

Nitrofurantoin is listed. Strides' US portfolio covers antibiotics and antivirals more broadly, so name any other molecule in your enquiry and the partnering team will confirm availability.

Why is nitrofurantoin used only for bladder infections?

Nitrofurantoin is cleared rapidly into urine and reaches very low concentrations in blood and tissues, so it works in the bladder but not for kidney or bloodstream infections.
