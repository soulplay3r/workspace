About this therapy area

Vitamin and electrolyte products correct deficiencies that are common in hospital and community practice: low potassium from diuretics or gastrointestinal losses, and low vitamin D from limited sun exposure, malabsorption or kidney disease. They look simple, but the dosage form matters. Extended-release potassium forms were developed to avoid gut injury, and high-strength vitamin D needs a stable oil-based formulation.

Strides has a broad potassium chloride franchise in the US, covering extended-release capsules and tablets, powder for oral solution and oral solution, and it supplies vitamin D products as soft gelatin capsules.

Molecule summaries

Ergocalciferol

Ergocalciferol is vitamin D2, a prohormone made by ultraviolet irradiation of ergosterol from yeast and fungi. It is converted in the liver and kidney to active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, which increases intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate. High-strength 50,000 IU capsules are used for hypoparathyroidism, refractory rickets and familial hypophosphataemia, and widely for vitamin D deficiency under supervision.

Potassium Chloride

Potassium chloride is an electrolyte replacement used to treat and prevent low blood potassium (hypokalaemia), most often caused by diuretics. Oral extended-release forms release potassium slowly to avoid the gut ulceration seen with early concentrated tablets, and liquid forms suit patients who cannot swallow tablets.

Frequently asked questions

Which potassium chloride dosage forms does Strides have?

Strides has reported a potassium chloride franchise in the US that includes extended-release capsules, extended-release tablets, powder for oral solution and oral solution.

Is ergocalciferol the same as vitamin D3?

No. Ergocalciferol is vitamin D2, made from plant and fungal sterols. Cholecalciferol is vitamin D3. Both are converted to active hormone in the liver and kidney.
