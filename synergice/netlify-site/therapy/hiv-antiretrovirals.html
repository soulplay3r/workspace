About this therapy area

Fixed-dose combinations changed HIV treatment in low- and middle-income countries. Putting three antiretrovirals in one daily tablet improves adherence, simplifies procurement and reduces cost, and most first-line treatment in these markets is now supplied by generic manufacturers, many of them in India.

Strides has a long history in antiretrovirals supplied through donor-funded and public procurement programmes. This section covers the two backbone regimens that have defined first-line treatment: tenofovir, lamivudine and efavirenz (TLE), and its successor, tenofovir, lamivudine and dolutegravir (TLD), which the World Health Organization has recommended as the preferred first-line regimen since 2019.

Molecule summaries

Tenofovir DF / Lamivudine / Dolutegravir

Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate, lamivudine and dolutegravir (TLD) is a once-daily single tablet combining two nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors with the integrase inhibitor dolutegravir. WHO has recommended TLD as the preferred first-line HIV treatment for adults and adolescents since 2019, and it is now used by most people on HIV treatment in low- and middle-income countries.

Tenofovir DF / Lamivudine / Efavirenz

Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate, lamivudine and efavirenz (TLE) is a once-daily, single-tablet regimen that combines two nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors with a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor. It was the WHO-preferred first-line HIV treatment from 2013 until dolutegravir-based TLD replaced it in 2019, and it remains an alternative regimen.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between TLE and TLD?

Both combine tenofovir disoproxil fumarate and lamivudine. TLE adds efavirenz, a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor. TLD adds dolutegravir, an integrase inhibitor with a higher barrier to resistance and better tolerability, which is why WHO now prefers TLD.

Are these products available for tender supply?

Antiretrovirals are often bought through national tenders and donor-funded programmes that require WHO prequalification or a stringent regulatory approval. Ask the partnering team for the product's current status.
