About this therapy area

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death worldwide, and the molecules used to manage it are among the most widely prescribed medicines in every market. Most are now off-patent and supplied as generics, so partners compare suppliers on evidence and reliability rather than on the molecule itself.

The cardiovascular molecules listed here cover the main pillars of long-term risk management: lowering LDL cholesterol (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin), preventing platelet-driven thrombosis (clopidogrel), and controlling blood pressure, angina and heart rate (amlodipine, metoprolol). Each molecule page sets out when the molecule was first approved, how it works and the facts a regulatory or sourcing team checks first.

Molecule summaries

Amlodipine

Amlodipine is a long-acting dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that relaxes arterial smooth muscle by blocking L-type calcium channels. Approved by the US FDA in July 1992 as Norvasc, it is used once daily for hypertension, chronic stable angina and vasospastic angina.

Atorvastatin

Atorvastatin is a synthetic statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by competitively inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in the liver's cholesterol synthesis. It was first approved by the US FDA in December 1996 as Lipitor and is used to treat hyperlipidaemia and to reduce the risk of heart attack and stroke.

Clopidogrel

Clopidogrel is an oral antiplatelet prodrug that irreversibly blocks the P2Y12 ADP receptor on platelets. Approved by the US FDA in November 1997 as Plavix, it is used to prevent heart attack and stroke after acute coronary syndrome, recent myocardial infarction or stroke, and in peripheral arterial disease.

Metoprolol

Metoprolol is a beta-1 selective beta blocker that slows the heart rate and reduces the force of contraction. First approved in the US in 1978 as metoprolol tartrate (Lopressor), and later as extended-release metoprolol succinate (Toprol-XL), it is used for hypertension, angina, after myocardial infarction and in heart failure.

Rosuvastatin

Rosuvastatin is a hydrophilic statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase in the liver. Discovered by Shionogi and developed by AstraZeneca as Crestor, it was approved by the US FDA in August 2003 and is the most potent statin per milligram in common use.
