About this therapy area

Transplant patients take immunosuppressants for life, usually as a combination of a calcineurin inhibitor, an antiproliferative agent and a corticosteroid. These are narrow therapeutic index medicines: small changes in exposure can mean rejection on one side or toxicity on the other, so regulators and clinicians scrutinise generic versions closely.

Strides has built an immunosuppressant portfolio around the three most widely used molecules: mycophenolate mofetil, tacrolimus and cyclosporine. The molecule pages below explain how each one blocks T-cell activation, how they are combined in practice, and the pharmacokinetic facts that matter for switching and therapeutic drug monitoring.

Molecule summaries

Cyclosporine

Cyclosporine (ciclosporin) is a cyclic peptide calcineurin inhibitor that suppresses T-cell activation. Discovered at Sandoz from the fungus Tolypocladium inflatum and approved by the US FDA in November 1983 as Sandimmune, it made routine organ transplantation possible and is also used in severe psoriasis and rheumatoid arthritis.

Mycophenolate Mofetil

Mycophenolate mofetil is a prodrug of mycophenolic acid, which blocks the enzyme IMPDH and stops lymphocytes from making the guanine nucleotides they need to multiply. Approved by the US FDA in May 1995 as CellCept, it is used with a calcineurin inhibitor and corticosteroids to prevent rejection after kidney, heart and liver transplantation.

Tacrolimus

Tacrolimus is a calcineurin inhibitor that blocks T-cell activation by preventing interleukin-2 transcription. Discovered in 1984 from the soil bacterium Streptomyces tsukubaensis and approved by the US FDA in April 1994 as Prograf, it is the most widely used immunosuppressant for preventing rejection after solid organ transplantation.

Frequently asked questions

Which immunosuppressants are in the Strides portfolio?

Strides has reported a US immunosuppressant portfolio that includes mycophenolate mofetil tablets and capsules, tacrolimus capsules and cyclosporine softgel capsules. Contact the partnering team for the position in your market.

Why do transplant immunosuppressants need extra care when switching supplier?

Tacrolimus and cyclosporine have narrow therapeutic indices and variable absorption, so blood levels are monitored after any change of product. Partners usually ask for bioequivalence data and plan switch protocols with prescribers.
